How to pick the answer when two look right

  1. Guarantees come from code, not prompts. If the question says "must never" or "downstream rejects", pick validation, filtering, hooks, schemas or strict tool use over "add it to the system prompt".
  2. Distrust absolutes. Options with "any", "always", "entirely", "in every context" or "only" are usually distractors.
  3. Diagnose before you fix. For debugging questions, the first step is to look at traces/logs of the failing case, not to change temperature, switch models or retry.
  4. Pick the simplest thing that meets the stated requirements. No portability need means no abstraction layer; a fixed sequence of steps means a workflow, not an autonomous agent.
  5. Prefer staged, tested, reversible change: regression tests, then migrate in batches, then assess downstream impact, then roll out.
  6. Least privilege and blast radius: separate keys per environment, read-only credentials, human approval for irreversible actions.
  7. Name the concept correctly. Prompt caching caches input prefixes, not outputs. Streaming changes delivery, not cost. Batches cut cost, not latency.
